# TMT — a scalar-tensor theory with double-well potential

> **About this site.** This site documents the development of a
> scalar-tensor extension of general relativity. The historical name
> "Mastery of Time" reflects the project's informal name in its early
> phase; the formal scientific designation is the
> **Temporon-Mediated Theory (TMT)**. Peer-reviewed publications are listed
> in the [Publications](publications.md) section.

## Overview

The **Temporon-Mediated Theory (TMT)** is a covariant extension of general
relativity in which a single real scalar field, the *temporon*
\( \psi \), is non-minimally coupled to the Ricci scalar through the
interaction \( -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\xi\psi^{2}R \) and endowed with a double-well
potential

$$ V(\psi) = \frac{\lambda}{4}\,(\psi^{2}-v^{2})^{2}. $$

The double-well structure generates two physically distinct regimes: a
**symmetric phase** \( \psi \to 0 \) in low-density or high-curvature
environments, and a **condensed phase** \( \psi \to \pm v \) in dense
baryonic environments. The non-minimal coupling makes the effective
gravitational coupling depend on the local condensate,
\( G_{\rm eff}(\psi) = G/(1-8\pi G\xi\psi^{2}) \).

The project pursues a precise and bounded goal: **to derive, from the
action, the effective mass profile of disk galaxies** and to confront that
derivation with observational data.

## Main result

In the static post-Newtonian limit, the action yields an effective mass
profile

$$ M_{\rm eff}(r) = M_{\rm bary}(r)\,\bigl[\,1 + (r/r_{c})^{n}\,\bigr], $$

and the exponential-disk geometry implies the scaling
\( r_{c}\propto M_{\rm bary}^{1/2} \). Tested against the SPARC sample of
**168 analysable disk galaxies**, this prediction reproduces the observed
empirical relation \( r_{c}\propto M_{\rm bary}^{0.48\pm0.04} \), in
agreement with the predicted exponent \( 1/2 \) at the \( 0.6\sigma \)
level.

The full derivation and validation are described in the
[Galactic results](resultats-galactiques.md) section.

## Current status

A manuscript presenting this derivation and its SPARC validation has been
**submitted to *Physical Review D* (May 2026)**. The cosmological
consequences of the same action — density-dependent late-time expansion,
the acoustic-peak structure of the cosmic microwave background, merging
clusters — are the subject of a **companion paper in preparation**. See the
[Limitations and open questions](limitations.md) section for an honest
account of what remains to be established.
