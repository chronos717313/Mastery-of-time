### The Problem: 95% of the Universe Remains Unexplained

The dominant cosmological model, known as ΛCDM (Lambda Cold Dark Matter), postulates that the universe is composed of approximately 5% ordinary visible matter, 27% dark matter (whose nature remains unknown), and 68% dark energy (also unexplained). Despite decades of intensive research and billions of dollars in experiments — including the Large Hadron Collider (CERN), underground liquid xenon detectors (LZ Collaboration, 2024), and gamma-ray observatories (Fermi-LAT) — no dark matter particle has ever been directly detected. Furthermore, the "Hubble tension" (5σ) reveals a persistent incompatibility between the two primary methods used to measure the expansion rate of the universe.
