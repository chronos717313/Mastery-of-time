Subject: A rotation curve framework that may surprise you — and I believe you are the right person to test it

---

Dear Professor Bovy,

I hesitated before writing to you, knowing how precious your time is. But after months of work and results that continue to surprise me, I concluded that some findings deserve to be shared — even imperfectly — rather than held back indefinitely for fear of being wrong.

I am an independent researcher, and I have developed a framework called the Theory of Time Mastery (TMT). Its central idea is both simple and audacious: the gravitational potential does not only curve space — it generates a measurable temporal distortion that contributes an additional effective mass to galactic dynamics, without invoking any exotic particle.

When I applied this framework to the SPARC catalogue — the same 175 galaxies of Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert 2016 that you know intimately — I did not expect what I found. Of 156 applicable galaxies, every single one was better described by TMT than by Newton alone, with a median chi² improvement of 97.5%. The transition radius r_c follows a beautifully clean mass-dependent law:

```
r_c(M) = 2.6 × (M/10¹⁰)^0.56 kpc     [r = 0.768, p = 3×10⁻²¹, 103 galaxies]
```

Across 8 independent observational tests — rotation curves, weak lensing, COSMOS2015, Pantheon+ SNIa, the Hubble tension — the combined statistical significance reaches p = 10⁻¹¹². I am aware that such a number demands extraordinary scrutiny, which is exactly why I am writing to you.

Your textbook "Dynamics and Astrophysics of Galaxies" and your APOGEE-based inference of the Milky Way's dark matter halo represent the most rigorous work I know on precisely this question. I would be deeply honoured — and genuinely challenged — to see what your expertise reveals when applied to TMT's predictions for our own galaxy. The Milky Way, with its well-constrained baryonic mass, is the one test I have not yet performed.

I am not asking you to believe these results. I am asking you to look at them with your most critical eye, and tell me where they fail.
