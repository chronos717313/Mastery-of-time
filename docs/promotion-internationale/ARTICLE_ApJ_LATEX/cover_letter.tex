Dear Editors,

I am submitting for your consideration the manuscript titled
\textbf{``Temporal Distortion as a Unified Explanation for Dark Matter
and Dark Energy: Validation Against Eight Independent Cosmological
Datasets,''} for publication in \textit{The Astrophysical Journal}.

\textbf{Summary of the work.}
I present the Theory of Time Mastery (TMT), which proposes that the
apparent effects of both dark matter and dark energy arise from the
local distortion of time in a gravitational field --- a consequence
already implicit in general relativity --- rather than from exotic
undiscovered particles or fields.  The key new result is an empirical
power-law correlation between the transition radius $r_c$ and baryonic
mass, $r_c \propto M^{0.56}$, confirmed with Pearson $r = 0.768$ and
$p = 3\times10^{-21}$ across 103 SPARC galaxies.  This relation is
absent from $\Lambda$CDM and constitutes a directly falsifiable
prediction.  Validated against eight independent datasets comprising
more than $2.4$ million galaxies and 1,700 Type~Ia supernovae, the
combined statistical significance is $p = 10^{-112}$.

\textbf{Significance and scope.}
The rotation curve problem and the Hubble tension are among the most
actively discussed unresolved questions in observational cosmology.
TMT addresses both within a single framework, without invoking any
particle beyond the Standard Model.  The $r_c(M)$ prediction can be
tested immediately with existing DESI and \textit{Euclid} data,
requiring no recalibration.
