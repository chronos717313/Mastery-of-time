# Frequently asked questions

> The historical name "Mastery of Time" reflects the project's informal
> name; the formal scientific designation is the
> **Temporon-Mediated Theory (TMT)**.

### Why is the site called "Mastery of Time"?

It is the project's informal name, inherited from its early development
phase. The formal scientific designation is the *Temporon-Mediated Theory*
(TMT), a scalar-tensor theory with a double-well potential. The rigorous
covariant formulation is given in the manuscript submitted to *Physical
Review D*.

### How does TMT differ from MOND?

MOND postulates a universal acceleration scale \( a_{0} \). TMT predicts a
**mass-dependent length scale**, \( r_{c}\propto M_{\rm bary}^{1/2} \),
derived from the action. This prediction is consistent with the SPARC
empirical exponent \( 0.48\pm0.04 \) at the \( 0.6\sigma \) level.

### Has this work been peer-reviewed?

A manuscript is under review at *Physical Review D* (submitted May 2026).
Earlier exploratory materials available on Zenodo and on this site reflect
the development history of the framework; they do not replace the
peer-reviewed publication.

### What is the temporon?

It is the scalar field \( \psi \) at the heart of the theory — the single
new degree of freedom introduced. It is non-minimally coupled to curvature
and has a double-well potential, which lets it exist in two distinct phases
depending on the local density.

### Does TMT eliminate dark matter?

At galactic scales, the temporon contribution reproduces rotation curves
without a dark-matter halo. At the scale of merging clusters,
compatibility remains an **open question** requiring a density-dependent
coupling, currently under analysis (see
[Limitations and open questions](limitations.md)). The theory therefore
does not claim, at this stage, to replace dark matter at all scales.

The calibration script and the data needed to reproduce the SPARC fits are
available in the GitHub repository
[`chronos717313/Mastery-of-time`](https://github.com/chronos717313/Mastery-of-time)
and archived on Zenodo. See the [Publications](publications.md) section.
